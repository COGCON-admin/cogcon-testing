What We're Measuring

♥

Heart Rate (Beat to Beat)

What it is

Beat-to-beat heart rate derived from the time between consecutive heartbeats (RR intervals), not a rolling average.

Why it matters

Beat-to-beat data reveals the variability pattern — the natural fluctuation in time between beats that indicates how well the autonomic nervous system is regulating under stress.

What to look for

A jagged, variable trace indicates good regulation. A flat, rigid trace at high HR indicates stress dominance.

〜

HR Coherence

What it is

A measure of heart rate variability derived from the rolling 60-beat RMSSD of RR intervals, normalised to a 0–100% scale.

Why it matters

Coherence reflects autonomic nervous system regulation. High coherence = regulated. Low coherence = stress dominating, cognitive performance at risk.

What to look for

Green trace (≥60%) = regulated. Amber (20–59%) = at risk. Red (<20%) = dysregulated. Watch for colour shifts at scenario entry.

↑

EPOC (Estimated)

What it is

Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption — accumulated physiological cost estimated from heart rate data.

Why it matters

Rising EPOC under moderate physical activity indicates stress activation is compounding the physical demand.

What to look for

Rapid EPOC rise at scenario entry = high stress activation. Sustained elevation = accumulated load affecting cognitive capacity.

Note

Precise EPOC available post-session from Firstbeat XLSX export.

⚡

SEB Windows

What it is

Periods where coherence drops below 30% — automatically detected and flagged in real time. Activates immediately and clears as soon as coherence returns to 30% or above.

Why it matters

SEB windows are retrospective coaching anchors — where SEB would have had highest impact.

What to look for

Green bar on left edge = SEB window active. Count and timing drives the retrospective debrief.

HR Trace Colour Thresholds

GREEN — Regulated (60–100%)

Coherence ≥ 60% — Autonomic regulation stable. Cognitive capacity protected. Optimal performance state.

AMBER — At Risk (20–59%)

Coherence 20–59% — Regulation under increasing load. SEB here prevents further dysregulation. SEB window active below 30%.

RED — Dysregulated (0–19%)

Coherence < 20% — Working memory and decision quality impaired. Immediate SEB required.

Note: Colour is coherence, height is HR. A red trace at moderate HR is more concerning than an amber trace at high HR.

SEB Cycle — Operational Reset Tool

S

Self Check

Rapid internal awareness check: heart rate, emotion, focus, information overload. Creates space between stimulus and response.

E

Eyes Up

Deliberate widening of the attentional field. Breaks fixation. Restores scanning and situational awareness.

B

Breathing & Body Language

Tactical breath to downshift arousal. Body language as performance control — squared, upright, deliberate.

OPS

Operational Performance Check

Am I in an optimal Operational Performance State? If yes — continue. If no — run SEB again before acting.

WIN

What's Important Now

Priority anchor. Compresses attention onto the next correct action. Bridge between regulation and decision execution.

Pre-Scenario Default Protocol

1. Execute SEB the moment job comms come through — by default, not when stress is noticed.

2. Apply multiple cycles in the truck if stress or worry builds during the wait.

3. Final SEB in the last 90 seconds before exiting the truck.

4. Identify WIN before the door opens.
